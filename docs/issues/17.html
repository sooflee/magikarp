Undercurrent

A federal judge calls a Flock licence-plate search 'indiscriminate mass surveillance', and London's station face scans catch nobody.

On October 1 Judge Sara Hill of the federal court in Tulsa threw out the evidence from a traffic stop after a sheriff's deputy searched FlockAn American company that sells networked licence-plate cameras to police departments and private customers.'s licence-plate camera network without a warrant. She called the system 'indiscriminate mass surveillance' that 'collects information about all vehicles that pass by any network-connected camera at all times'. TechCrunch called it one of the first federal rulings against a Flock search. The next day Senator Bernie Sanders introduced the Ban Flock Act, which would bar federal agencies from using automatic licence-plate readers. In London, a freedom-of-information request reported by the Guardian on September 29 showed a six-month trial of live facial recognition at railway stations scanned more than half a million faces, produced one alert, a false match, and no arrests.

Deep-dive of the weekstalling

Medicare's first final mandatory rule tying drug prices to other countries comes out with less than a twenty-fifth of its promised savings.

On September 30 the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services finalised GLOBEThe Global Benchmark for Efficient Drug Pricing model, a Medicare experiment that sets drugmakers' Part B rebates against prices in other rich countries., a model that ties the rebates drugmakers owe on some Part BThe part of traditional Medicare that pays for outpatient care, including drugs given in a doctor's office or clinic, such as many cancer infusions. drugs to prices in 19 other rich countries. Its projected seven-year savings fell to $440 million, from $11.9 billion in the proposal, and the cut in patients' cost-sharing from $1.4 billion to $50 million, according to the American Action Forum's reading of the rule. It covers about a quarter of traditional Medicare patients, in randomly chosen areas, starting in January 2027, and STAT reports that only four companies are expected to be caught, because drugmakers that signed voluntary pricing deals with the administration are exempt. The companion model for pharmacy drugs, GUARD, is still a proposal. Medicare's administrator, Mehmet OzThe administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services since 2025, a former heart surgeon and television host., gave no savings figure in the announcement.

Key factCMS's own estimate assumes drugmakers will raise prices abroad in response, cutting what GLOBE collects by 60 percent from 2030, according to the American Action Forum.

What the readings mean

Volatility measures how much the market is expected to move in the near term compared with the longer term, so a lower reading means less immediate stress. The yield curve is the gap between long-term and short-term government borrowing rates, and a steep curve usually points to expected growth rather than recession. When crypto is described as risk-off, investors are stepping back from the most speculative assets, which often serves as an early note of caution beneath a calm market.

The wildcard · Education

School blockades close nearly one French lycée in ten, and pupils turn down the education minister's offer.

On October 2 nearly 400 of France's roughly 3,700 lycéesFrench upper-secondary schools, for pupils aged roughly 15 to 18, ending in the baccalauréat exam. were closed, Reuters reported, after blockades that began in the Paris suburbs in mid-September spread across the country and onto university campuses. Justice Minister Gérald DarmaninFrance's justice minister, previously interior minister. put arrests since the protests began at 2,000, most of them teenagers, though the interior minister gave higher counts. Pupils complain of classes of 35, broken buildings and teachers who are absent and not replaced, as the government's draft 2027 budget raises education spending 1.7 percent. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu held a crisis meeting on October 1. On October 2 Education Minister Édouard Geffray offered a plan to replace absent teachers faster; the main pupils' union rejected it and urged pupils to join a national day of action on October 6.

Student protests grip France as Prime Minister Lecornu calls crisis meeting (AP via PBS NewsHour, October 1)
AP reports many dozens of protesters and police injured and quotes Geffray: 'Violence will not deprive our students of their fundamental right to education.' He ordered remote classes where blockades or damage put pupils at risk.

Violence spreads as French school blockades enter second week (Brussels Signal, October 2)
It dates the first blockades to September 21 and counts 1,027 lycées disrupted on October 1, with 305 police and gendarmes injured that day. Its figures come from French officials and media, and arrest counts vary widely between outlets.

Blocage des lycées: un vendredi sous haute tension, les syndicats reçus par le ministre (Sud Radio, October 2)
