AI & compute

Signals to watch

Inference costs reverse toward custom silicon · active regime
After years of collapsing inference prices, the newest frontier models are raising them, pushing companies from general-purpose GPUs toward custom chips. AMD agreed on August 6 to buy Taalas, which burns a model's weights into the chip's own wiring: its HC1 encodes Llama 3.1 8B across 53 billion transistors and is quoted near 17,000 tokens per second per user at about 200 watts.
What to watch: Nvidia notified customers on August 22 of price rises above 15 percent on AI servers shipping early next year, blaming memory: server DRAM doubled in the first quarter and is now about a quarter of high-end rack cost, and Micron, SK Hynix and Samsung hold the pricing power. Watch whether the next price moves come from HBM contracts rather than GPU lists. Where it shows up: AMD, Marvell, Broadcom, and other custom-silicon makers.
Crypto leverage & dry powder · active (squeeze)
Leverage in crypto futures and stablecoin dry powder swing sharply, and extreme readings often precede reversals.
What to watch: Bitcoin ended the week up 24 percent at $78,000 after $1.44 billion of shorts liquidated on August 19, the kind of reversal this card exists to catch. Watch whether futures leverage and funding rates rebuild toward the extremes that preceded the spring drawdown, and whether the CLARITY Act contract, still at 16.5 cents, follows the price. Where it shows up: Bitcoin, Ether, and Coinbase.

Replying 'AI;DR' is becoming an acceptable answer to pasted model text.

Undercurrent

Rick Manelius coined 'AI;DR' on August 17 for the reply you give unedited model output: if the sender did not bother to edit it, the reader will not bother to read it. The post reached number one on Hacker News at 1,111 points, and a companion manifesto, dontpastetheai.com, drew 1,044 points for asking people to answer questions in their own words, in the tradition of nohello.net. Both landed the same week Responsible Statecraft caught a government paying $900,000 for exactly the kind of text readers are learning to skip.

  • AI;DR (AI; Didn't Read)
  • Don't paste the AI, please
The wider world

China scrubs its moon-water launch on the pad, and lab-grown brain tissue turns out to keep time.steady

Deep-dive of the week

China rolled Chang'e-7 to the pad at Wenchang on August 19 and announced on August 23 that the probe 'does not meet launch conditions' and cannot fly in the planned window this year, after a tropical storm closed the range across a narrow trans-lunar window. The mission is the flagship robotic hunt for water ice near the lunar south pole: an orbiter, lander, rover and a hopping probe aimed at the Shackleton crater region. The same week, Harvard reported cortical organoids alive since 2018 whose cells aged on the body's schedule, and TU Wien began building an electron microscope with a quantum computer inside it.

  • China abruptly postpones ambitious Chang'e-7 lunar mission launch (Phys.org)
  • Human brain organoids record the passage of time over multiple years (Nature)
  • Quantum computer microscope set to improve electron microscopy (Phys.org)
  • JWST finds hidden stars in the universe's most ancient galaxies (Space.com)

Officials told international partners the Chang'e-7 slip is at least a month, with backup windows from late September into 2027; SpaceNews read the official language as a possible slip to next year, and Blue Origin's Blue Moon Mk1 is aimed at the same south-pole region in the same period.

Fifty percent tariffs land on Canada as the talks collapse over final terms.elevated

Geopolitics

Trade talks in Washington broke down late on August 21, and 50 percent tariffs on about $20 billion of Canadian goods took effect the next morning, with energy, potash and critical minerals carved out. Carney set dollar-for-dollar retaliation for September 8 and, in NPR's account, said Canada is economically 'at war' with the United States. In Kyiv, an overnight barrage of missiles and 168 drones on August 19 killed 17 people, 15 of them in one residential building, while fuel rationing spread across more than half of Russia's regions. New Zealand moved to ban under-16s from social media, Uzbekistan appeared to take delivery of Chinese fighter jets, and Pakistan's Supreme Court fought the government over a jailed former prime minister's heart exam.

US-Canada talks collapse and 50 percent tariffs take effect (Bloomberg)
Talks broke down late on August 21 after what a Canadian envoy called changes to the 'fine print'; the duties cover about $20 billion of goods including alcohol, dairy and cement, with energy, potash and critical minerals carved out. Trump wrote on August 23 that 'Canada wants the benefits of being a State, without being one'. The trade-fragmentation regime now runs through North America's most integrated border.

Russian missiles and 168 drones kill 17 in Kyiv as air defences run low (Moscow Times)
Fifteen of the dead were in one nine-story building; Zelenskyy said Patriot interceptors are 'needed every day', then rejected calls for a wartime election as a 'tsunami' that could split the country. On the other side, fuel was available at 28.1 percent of Russian stations on August 16, down from 41 a week earlier, and Putin threatened Ukraine's 'most sensitive economic sectors' in return. The war is now an energy war in both directions.

New Zealand moves to ban under-16s from social media (Bloomberg)
Luxon's Online Safety Bill, announced August 24 in Wellington, would fine 'high risk' platforms up to 10 percent of global revenue for failing to check ages by ID, facial estimation or account data; both coalition partners oppose it and a first reading this term looks unlikely. The checking machinery is the age-assurance infrastructure already on our watchlist.

Uzbekistan appears to take delivery of Chinese J-10CE fighters (Eurasianet)
Eurasianet reports four Chengdu J-10CE fighters delivered under an agreement for a reported 24, which neither government has confirmed, and frames the deal as China filling the arms-supply vacuum left by Russia's war, with cooperation widening in Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Turkmenistan. Central Asia's security guarantees used to run through Moscow.

UN rights chief tells Zambia to halt arbitrary arrests of opposition figures (UN News)
Last week's deep-dive ended with the count suspended and the army at the tally centres. Hichilema was declared winner near 60 percent; police arrested 11 people including senior opposition figures on election night over an alleged insurrection plot, and Volker Turk called for due process on August 19. Mundubile calls the plot a fabrication and has moved to what his party calls a secure location.

Pakistan's top court orders Imran Khan to hospital; the government sends him back to jail (Al Jazeera)
The Supreme Court ordered the 73-year-old former prime minister moved to Shifa International within two days, after a jail cardiac board recorded fluctuating blood pressure and severe anxiety. The government instead took him briefly to a state hospital on August 20 and returned him to Adiala prison, and his party says the ordered heart scan was never performed. He has been imprisoned since 2023.

The Treasury starts buying twice as many of its own long bonds, and gold and bitcoin rise while stocks fall.

Markets

The 30-year Treasury yield touched its highest since 2007 early in the week, and on August 19 the Treasury answered: buyback operations in 10- to 30-year debt roughly double, to $4 billion and more per operation, from September 9. Ten-year yields fell 6 basis points that day, the dollar ended the week down 0.9 percent, gold closed a third straight weekly gain at a three-month high, and bitcoin rose 24 percent in a squeeze that liquidated $1.44 billion of short positions after the SEC proposed its crypto rulebook and Trump pushed the CLARITY Act. Stocks did not join: the S&P 500 fell 1.4 percent, its first weekly loss since late July, with the flash services PMI hot at 56.8 and July's Fed minutes showing 'several' members beyond the three dissenters wanting a hike. Volatility measures how much the market is expected to move in the near term compared with the longer term, so a lower reading means less immediate stress. The yield curve is the gap between long-term and short-term government borrowing rates, and a steep curve usually points to expected growth rather than recession. When crypto is described as risk-off, investors are stepping back from the most speculative assets, which often serves as an early note of caution beneath a calm market.

Shein prices its listing at a quarter of its 2022 valuation and volunteers for a US security review.

The wildcard · Companies and deals

Shein set September 1 as the target for its Hong Kong debut, seeking up to about $1.8 billion at a valuation near $27 billion, down from $98 billion in its 2022 private round; planned London and New York listings never happened, and UBS Asset Management joined as a cornerstone investor after Reuters reported soft demand. On August 23 Bloomberg reported the company voluntarily asked CFIUS, the US national-security review panel, to examine its $80 million purchase of the American retailer Everlane, a filing companies almost never make after a deal has closed, because clearance would shield its US expansion from a later unwind. The prospectus shows 2025 profit down 38.7 percent to $2.06 billion after the US and EU ended duty-free small parcels, the cost advantage the company was built on.
