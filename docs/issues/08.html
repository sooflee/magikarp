Signals to watch

Inference costs reverse toward custom silicon · active regime
After years of collapsing inference prices, the newest frontier models are raising them, pushing companies from general-purpose GPUs toward custom chips. AMD agreed on August 6 to buy Taalas, which burns a model's weights into the chip's own wiring: its HC1 encodes Llama 3.1 8B across 53 billion transistors and is quoted near 17,000 tokens per second per user at about 200 watts.
What to watch: Whether a model-specific chip can be turned fast enough to matter. Taalas customises two of roughly 100 metal layers and quotes about two months at TSMC per model, against a frontier release cadence of weeks. Where it shows up: AMD, Marvell, Broadcom, and other custom-silicon makers.

Frontier results now arrive as announcements, not papers.

Undercurrent

Science reported this week that the leading AI startups publish almost none of their research, a sharp break from the field's open norms of a decade ago. Then OpenAI illustrated the new mode on August 1: ten claimed advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science, credited to an internal model called Astra that no one outside can use, shipped with Lean proof certificates instead of peer review. Greg Brockman put the compute cost near $2,000 at API rates. The proofs are checkable; the system that produced them is not.

The wider world

CXMT's 472 percent debut shows where the stack is winning; zombie firms show what it drags.accelerating

Deep-dive of the week

Memory-chip maker CXMT opened its Shanghai debut on July 27 up 472 percent from its IPO price, briefly China's most valuable listed company. The financial layer underneath is deleveraging: Shanghai margin traders cut leveraged positions about 14 percent over July, brokerage data show. Two ChinaTalk essays this week mapped the structural edges. One argues China's real bottleneck is zombie firms that subsidies keep alive. The other warns the West is fixated on who owns mines while China builds the automation that runs them: 72 percent of battery-powered autonomous haul trucks operate in China.

A Gaza disarmament roadmap lands as Europe's borders and rivers both give way.elevated

Geopolitics

Mediators from Egypt, Qatar, Turkiye and the US announced late on July 30 that Hamas has agreed to disarm in stages against a phased Israeli withdrawal, with a detailed roadmap due within 14 days. Israel has not publicly signed on, and one Israeli minister called the deal unacceptable. The same week, thousands crossed from Morocco into Ceuta and Spain deployed the military, Ukraine's drones burned Wildberries warehouses in its costliest strike on Russian commercial infrastructure, and the Danube fell so low that Hungary's only nuclear plant stopped generating for the first time in 44 years.

Gaza Board of Peace announces Hamas disarmament agreement: what we know
Staged disarmament against staged withdrawal, with heavy weapons on a 200-to-350-day clock; the first structured end-of-war framework either side has accepted.

Ukrainian drones kill 8 in Russia and strike Wildberries warehouse
The weekend wave on Russia's biggest online retailer killed eight; Russian analysts put cumulative damage near 100 billion rubles, the war's costliest campaign against commercial infrastructure.

Spain deploys military to Ceuta after migrant surge: what we know
Thousands crossed from Morocco in days and at least 18 died; France tightened its borders in response, and the free-movement argument moved to the EU's center.

Dry Danube threatens nuclear power in Romania and Hungary
Hungary's Paks plant, source of about half the country's power, stopped generating August 2 for the first time in 44 years; river-cooled reactors met a river that is no longer there.

Second phase of Kashmir elections concludes amid rigging allegations
PML-N swept eight of ten declared refugee seats in the August 2 vote after a summer of unrest that killed more than 30; landslides pushed several constituencies to August 4.

Japan earthquake death toll rises to 18 as rescue efforts continue
The July 28 Kumamoto quake killed at least 18. TSMC's Japanese fab reported no structural damage and resumed operations; Tokyo Electron halted plants while the chip supply chain checked itself.

The Fed holds 9 to 3, and the market moves the fight to September.

Markets

The Federal Reserve held rates at 3.5 to 3.75 percent on July 29, with three regional presidents dissenting for a quarter-point increase, the most same-direction dissents since 2016. Chair Warsh called it a family fight and kept the statement short. Kalshi's September contract ended the week pricing a hike as more likely than a hold. Equities recovered anyway on a split earnings week: Microsoft and Amazon jumped about 9 percent on their reports, Meta fell about 10 on a miss with capital spending raised again. The S&P 500 rose 1.05 percent to 7,489.72 and the VIX fell to 16. The Bank of Japan held at 1 percent with the yen near a 40-year low. Directional only, not investment advice.

Volatility measures how much the market is expected to move in the near term compared with the longer term, so a lower reading means less immediate stress. The yield curve is the gap between long-term and short-term government borrowing rates, and a steep curve usually points to expected growth rather than recession. When crypto is described as risk-off, investors are stepping back from the most speculative assets, which often serves as an early note of caution beneath a calm market.

FIFA tried to sell a piece of the World Cup, and Europe walked out until it stopped.

The wildcard · Football's capital revolt

On July 30, UEFA's 55 national associations voted unanimously to boycott all FIFA competitions, the World Cup included, over President Gianni Infantino's plan to sell stakes of up to 20 percent in FIFA's commercial enterprise to private investors, Joshua Kushner among them. The statement demanded the plan be abandoned entirely, with binding assurances it would never return. FIFA withdrew the proposal within two days, and by August 1 UEFA had declared the matter closed. A fight that could have split world football ended as the fastest institutional climbdown of the year.
