Kimi K3's weights land a day early, and the price war answers within four days.

Moonshot published Kimi K3's weights on the evening of July 26, a day ahead of its own deadline: 2.8 trillion parameters across 96 shards, about 1.4 terabytes, under a modified MIT license, the largest open release to date. Anthropic responded July 27 with a position paper saying it has never advocated a ban on open weightsModels whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download and run, even if the training data and code are not.. OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna's price 80 percent on July 30, and DeepSeek shipped V4-Flash-0731 on July 31 at an unchanged 14 cents per million input tokens. The threatened US restriction on Chinese open models has still not appeared.

Undercurrent

Frontier results now arrive as announcements, not papers.

Science reported this week that the leading AI startups publish almost none of their research, a sharp break from the field's open norms of a decade ago. Then OpenAI illustrated the new mode on August 1: ten claimed advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science, credited to an internal model called Astra that no one outside can use, shipped with Lean proof certificates instead of peer review. Greg Brockman put the compute cost near $2,000 at API rates. The proofs are checkable; the system that produced them is not.

The wider world

Deep-dive of the weekaccelerating

CXMT's 472 percent debut shows where the stack is winning; zombie firms show what it drags.

Memory-chip maker CXMT opened its Shanghai debut on July 27 up 472 percent from its IPO price, briefly China's most valuable listed company. The financial layer underneath is deleveraging: Shanghai margin traders cut leveraged positions about 14 percent over July, brokerage data show. Two ChinaTalk essays this week mapped the structural edges. One argues China's real bottleneck is zombie firms that subsidies keep alive. The other warns the West is fixated on who owns mines while China builds the automation that runs them: 72 percent of battery-powered autonomous haul trucks operate in China.

Key factClearing zombie-firm inefficiency would add 0.7 to 1.2 points of annual growth, IMF work cited by ChinaTalk estimates, roughly a fifth of China's target.

Geopoliticselevated

A Gaza disarmament roadmap lands as Europe's borders and rivers both give way.

Mediators from Egypt, Qatar, Turkiye and the US announced late on July 30 that Hamas has agreed to disarm in stages against a phased Israeli withdrawal, with a detailed roadmap due within 14 days. Israel has not publicly signed on, and one Israeli minister called the deal unacceptable. The same week, thousands crossed from Morocco into Ceuta and Spain deployed the military, Ukraine's drones burned Wildberries warehouses in its costliest strike on Russian commercial infrastructure, and the Danube fell so low that Hungary's only nuclear plant stopped generating for the first time in 44 years.

Key factThe disarmament agreement gives mediators 14 days from July 31, to about August 14, to produce the detailed roadmap.

Commodities & energy · July 31

The oil shock unwinds, and the grain bid breaks with it.

Brent gave back 6.9 percent to $90.12 and WTI 5.2 percent to $84.67 as Saudi Arabia floated a naval coalition to protect Gulf shipping, word spread that US-Iran talks would restart, and OPEC+OPEC plus allied producers such as Russia, which coordinate oil output targets. headed into Sunday's meeting expected to add supply. It did: 188,000 barrels a day for September, completing the rollback of the 2023 cuts. Grains broke too, corn, wheat and soybeans all down 5 to 6 percent, and natural gas fell 4.3. Coffee, the one riser, snapped a monthlong slide with a 5.8 percent gain. The forward question is whether $90 crude holds once the Iran talks actually convene.

What the readings mean

Volatility measures how much the market is expected to move in the near term compared with the longer term, so a lower reading means less immediate stress. The yield curve is the gap between long-term and short-term government borrowing rates, and a steep curve usually points to expected growth rather than recession. When crypto is described as risk-off, investors are stepping back from the most speculative assets, which often serves as an early note of caution beneath a calm market.

The wildcard · Football's capital revolt

FIFA tried to sell a piece of the World Cup, and Europe walked out until it stopped.

On July 30, UEFA's 55 national associations voted unanimously to boycott all FIFA competitions, the World Cup included, over President Gianni Infantino's plan to sell stakes of up to 20 percent in FIFA's commercial enterprise to private investors, Joshua Kushner among them. The statement demanded the plan be abandoned entirely, with binding assurances it would never return. FIFA withdrew the proposal within two days, and by August 1 UEFA had declared the matter closed. A fight that could have split world football ended as the fastest institutional climbdown of the year.

Inference costs reverse toward custom silicon · active regime
Memory, not logic, is now the scarce input: on September 30 Micron reported record quarterly revenue of $54.23 billion, against $11.32 billion a year earlier, and said more than 75 percent of its 2027 output is already committed.
What to watch: Micron expects memory supply-demand conditions to be 'much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026' and has no 'line of sight' to balance. Watch whether server makers keep shipping machines with less memory per server, and whether the labs' $2 and $10 list price survives the end of Google's introductory Argon price. Where it shows up: AMD, Marvell, Broadcom, Samsung, and SK Hynix.
Water as a grid constraint · watch (grid water)
Data-centre water use is treated as a trade secret, and the first numbers to escape show how much it varies: an improper redaction in Nebraska put Google's Lincoln campus at 13.3 million gallons a year and its Papillion site at 547.9 million.
What to watch: The Lincoln site also showed a 52.65 megawatt peak draw, KOLN reported on September 30. Watch whether cities start requiring the figures in development agreements. Where it shows up: European power prices, nuclear operators, and grid-interconnection queues.

AI sovereignty and open weights opening

Last week's test resolved. The biggest open release to date actually landed, on time and a day early, and Washington let it: the restriction weighed in July had produced no order or rule by August 2, and the most restriction-minded US lab published a position paper disclaiming any wish for a ban. The live question moves from whether the flood is real to whether distillation controls and safety-testing mandates, the asks Anthropic substituted for a ban, get traction in the fall.
