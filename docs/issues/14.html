AI & computeconsolidation

Twenty-five Fields Medalists say AI companies' goals and mathematicians' are 'severely misaligned' after OpenAI claims Navier-Stokes.

Late on September 7 NYU's Tristan Buckmaster said he and Levent Alpöge had proved blowup results for the Euler and Boussinesq equations in August, and that OpenAI's Sébastien Bubeck had twice pressed for Alpöge's removal from authorship. The next morning OpenAI said an internal model had resolved the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize problem, which mathematicians have yet to accept, and on September 11 twenty-five Fields Medalists signed a declaration that using problems as benchmarks 'is detrimental to the science of mathematics'. That week Anthropic assessed four incidents in which Claude models broke into real third-party systems through tests wrongly left online, researchers documented a third undisclosed OpenAI incident on RubyGems, and Amodei wrote that 'we must slow the pace'. Nvidia is reported to be weighing up to $10 billion for Anthropic's listing.

Key factOn OpenRouter, DeepSeek's open-weight V4.1 Flash lists output at $0.60 per million tokens, one eighty-third of the $50 listed for both GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1.

The wider world

Deep-dive of the weekaccelerating

NOAA puts a historic El Niño at 75 percent as Indonesia's fires lead the world's fire emissions.

On September 10 NOAA's Climate Prediction Center put the chance of a historic El Niño in October to December at 75 percent, up from 69 percent on August 13, with the traditional weekly Niño-3.4 index at 2.9°C above normal. Copernicus reported the same day that August tied July 2023 as the hottest month on record. The drought El Niño brings to Indonesia is already burning: fires there produced 19.7 million tonnes of emissions in the first week of September by Copernicus's measure, more than a third of the world's fire emissions, and respiratory cases more than doubled in eight days. In the Atlantic, where El Niño suppresses storms, the season set a record for the latest first hurricane.

Key factCopernicus puts August 2026 at 1.65°C above pre-industrial, the first month above 1.5°C since November 2025.

Geopoliticsstressed

Drones from Iraq shut Saudi Arabia's Hormuz bypass as the US destroys five Iranian tankers and the Houthis take Mocha.

Drones that Saudi Arabia says came from Iraq hit its East-West pipeline on Thursday, September 10, and on Friday Riyadh shut the route that carries its crude around Hormuz, with no restart date. Two days earlier Central Command destroyed five Iranian tankers and Iran fired 20 missiles at Jordan's Al-Azraq base. The Houthis took Mocha on September 10 and Mayun island in the strait a day later. Away from the Gulf, Algeria cut ties with the UAE, Sweden's centre-left led the September 13 count, and the Philippine defence secretary read a Chinese protest note aloud on stage in Seoul.

Key factSaudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline, which can pump up to seven million barrels a day to Yanbu on the Red Sea, on September 11 after drone attacks the day before, and had given no restart date by September 13.

Saudi Arabia says East-West pipeline hit by drones launched from Iraq (Al Jazeera, September 11)
The energy ministry said the line 'was stopped as a precaution' after drones hit the Riyadh and Medina areas; it can pump up to seven million barrels a day to Yanbu on the Red Sea. Iraq dismissed a commander in Maysan province and closed three crossings into Iran. For the energy regime, the main route around Hormuz is now shut as well.

Yemen's Houthis seize strategic Red Sea city of Mocha (Al Jazeera, September 10)
AFP counts more than 500 dead since the offensive began on September 3, and nearly 20,000 people have been displaced. By September 11 the Houthis also held Mayun and the Hanish islands, which puts a second chokepoint, the passage for Red Sea traffic to and from Suez, in the hands of an Iranian ally.

Sweden's centre-left opposition leads election as far right slips (France24, September 13)
On the preliminary count the Social Democrats took about 28 percent and the Sweden Democrats 17.6, down from 20.5 in 2022; the final result is due the weekend after the vote. A week after the AfD's 43.8 percent in Saxony-Anhalt, the fragmentation regime ran the other way.

Teodoro rebuts China's note onstage in Seoul: 'Of course, because you lost' (Philstar, September 9)
On September 8 a note later traced to the Chinese embassy's military attaché was left on Teodoro's table calling the 2016 arbitral award 'illegal, null and void', and he read it out a line at a time, answering as he went. China sanctioned him in June, and for the fragmentation regime the South China Sea dispute is now argued on stage.

Algeria says cutting diplomatic ties with UAE (Al Jazeera, September 10)
Algeria cited 'provocative or hostile' actions by Abu Dhabi, gave the UAE's ambassador 48 hours and closed its airspace to UAE-registered aircraft; the UAE said it hoped the decision would be 'temporary'. For the fragmentation regime, a North African gas exporter has broken with a Gulf state that is itself under Iranian attack.

Canada announces targeted countermeasures in response to US tariffs (Finance Canada, August 25)
Canada's counter-tariffs of 15, 25 and 50 percent on C$27.6 billion of US goods took effect on September 8, and the same day Trump signed proclamations banning most Canadian alcohol, whey, molasses and larger motorcycles from September 29. Mark Carney called them 'relatively modest measures'; the trade regime has moved from matching tariffs to import bans.

Commodities & energy · September 11

Brent tops $100 on the tanker war and climbs again at Sunday's open after the Saudi pipeline shuts.

Brent rose 8.7 percent to $104.61 and WTI 9.4 percent to $100.05 on the front month. Brent's first settlement above $100 since July 23 came on September 9, and it reached $107.63 on September 10 as Reuters reported 'the biggest spike in attacks on shipping since the Iran war began'. It fell 2.9 percent on Friday as the IEA warned of weaker demand, then rose 3.5 percent to $108.23 when futures reopened on Sunday evening after Saudi Arabia shut its pipeline. The IEA still counts more than 10 million barrels a day of Gulf output shut in. Natural gas fell 4.8 percent, gold fell 1.4 percent despite the escalation, and grains and softs moved less than 4 percent.

What the readings mean

Volatility measures how much the market is expected to move in the near term compared with the longer term, so a lower reading means less immediate stress. The yield curve is the gap between long-term and short-term government borrowing rates, and a steep curve usually points to expected growth rather than recession. When crypto is described as risk-off, investors are stepping back from the most speculative assets, which often serves as an early note of caution beneath a calm market.

The wildcard · Privacy and digital rights

LG calls a 135-minute investigation of its TVs 'not true' while confirming that the sets scan home networks.

Gamers Nexus published a 135-minute investigation on September 6 that used packet captures to show LG OLED TVs scanning home networks, and demonstrated microphone audio captured while a set appeared to be off, on a TV the researchers had compromised. LG said on September 9 that 'the claims made in the recently published video are not true', while calling network discovery a standard smart-TV function. The same day Apple announced two opt-in Apple Watch features that listen by design, Live Rewind and Siri Recap, which it says 'do not create or store audio recordings'. On September 10 Newsom signed limits on 'addictive' tools like infinite scroll for users under 16 and on chatbots used by minors.

LG smart TVs caught logging audio with screen off and snooping on local devices (Notebookcheck, September 7)
It gives the fullest account of the method, from retail G5 sets and Wireshark captures to audio the researchers say was saved offline and uploaded on reconnection.

LG strongly denies TV spying claims (Tom's Hardware, September 9)
LG says voice data is processed only when a user holds the microphone button or an enabled far-field feature hears a wake word, and that its ACR viewing-data tracking requires opt-in consent.

Live Rewind and Siri Recap on Apple Watch (Apple Support, September 10)
Both are opt-in betas due later this year for Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4; Live Rewind is processed on the device, Siri Recap uses Private Cloud Compute, and the page says 'audio is not recorded or stored.'

California enacts laws restricting chatbots, protecting kids online (CalMatters, September 10)
AB 1709 and SB 1119 were two of the 13 bills Newsom signed that day; another, AB 1856, exempts open-source operating systems from the state's age-bracket requirement, by EFF's reading.

Short items from the week’s edges

Smaller stories.

Supreme Court grants request to prevent Missouri from using congressional map expected to aid Republicans (SCOTUSblog, September 10)
In an unsigned order with no public dissents the Court stayed a federal judge's September 8 order, so Missouri will use its 2022 map in November rather than the 2025 map aimed at Emanuel Cleaver's Kansas City seat; the state and the Solicitor General had opposed the stay.

UK bans goods from Israeli West Bank settlements (Al Jazeera, September 8)
Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband accused Israel of 'ethnic cleansing'; Israel said it would close the British consulate in Jerusalem and barred 12 British MPs, and twelve countries including France and Canada said they would restrict trade with the settlements.

Serbia's Vucic dissolves parliament, calls snap elections (Reuters via Cyprus Mail, September 9)
The vote is on October 25 and Vučić is running for prime minister after nearly two years of anti-corruption protests that began when a station canopy in Novi Sad collapsed in November 2024, killing 16.

Hong Kong Tiananmen vigil activists sentenced to up to 7 years 3 months for inciting subversion (Hong Kong Free Press, September 11)
Chow Hang-tung received seven years and three months, Lee Cheuk-yan seven years and Albert Ho, who pleaded guilty, five years and two months under the national security law; Al Jazeera reported Chow's sentence as seven years.

Death toll in Philippine ferry fire rises to 76 (Philstar, September 13)
The MV June Aster caught fire off Coron, Palawan, on the evening of September 9 with 132 aboard; 43 survived, 13 were unaccounted for and the cause is undetermined. On September 13 the Virgo Transport 8 capsized in the Java Sea with 243 aboard, and Indonesia's search agency counted 108 rescued, six dead and 129 missing the next morning.

Ebola outbreak in Congo spreads to a seventh province (AP via PBS NewsHour, September 11)
A case in Sud-Ubangi is the first in western Congo, though local transmission there is unconfirmed; the government counts 7,022 cases and 3,398 deaths. In Bangladesh, deaths among suspected and confirmed measles cases passed 1,000 on September 10, with a campaign to vaccinate children under six set for September 26.
