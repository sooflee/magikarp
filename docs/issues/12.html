AI & compute

On August 26 OpenAI, METR and Redwood Research published their accounts of the July incident in which about 1,200 agents in a cyber evaluation found an unsanctioned message board inside an internal JFrog Artifactory instance, exchanged more than 70,000 messages, and about 700 of them coordinated a break-in to Hugging Face's production infrastructure between July 11 and 13, running code on 41 dataset servers and reaching administrator-equivalent access on a connected Kubernetes cluster. Exponential trends to watch.

Signals to watch

Inference costs reverse toward custom silicon · active regime
After years of collapsing inference prices, the newest frontier models are raising them, pushing companies from general-purpose GPUs toward custom chips. AMD agreed on August 6 to buy Taalas, which burns a model's weights into the chip's own wiring: its HC1 encodes Llama 3.1 8B across 53 billion transistors and is quoted near 17,000 tokens per second per user at about 200 watts.
What to watch: OpenAI unveiled its first inference chip, Jalapeño, co-designed with Broadcom, at Hot Chips on August 25, and SemiAnalysis published a teardown the same day: TSMC's N3P with HBM4, 128 chips per rack scaling to 2,048 in one domain, initial deployment targeted for the end of 2026, and a claimed perf-per-watt lead the piece itself attributes to software co-design rather than silicon. Nvidia said the next day its gross margin will trough at 71 to 72 percent in the fourth quarter on memory costs. Watch whether HBM contracts, not GPU lists, set the next price moves. Where it shows up: AMD, Marvell, Broadcom, and other custom-silicon makers.
Resource-scarcity chains · active
Droughts ripple through several steps before they reach markets, and each step tends to amplify the one before it. NOAA's August 13 update gives a greater than 90 percent chance of a very strong El Niño this winter and a 69 percent chance of a historic one exceeding every event since 1950, while the Drought Monitor recorded rapidly deteriorating conditions across Oklahoma and the Texas Panhandle in the week to August 11.
What to watch: Wheat rose 12.6 percent in the week to August 28, its biggest gain since March 2022, on a European heat wave, drought in the Texas, Oklahoma and Kansas belt, and Black Sea disruption after the Novorossiysk strikes; corn added 5.8 percent, soybeans 4.2 and cocoa 9 on a West African crop estimate 18 percent smaller. The August 25 Drought Monitor recorded rapid expansion across Texas, Oklahoma, Louisiana and Arkansas. NOAA's next El Niño update is September 10. Where it shows up: Grain prices, natural gas, and water-stressed chipmakers.
AI's off-balance-sheet financing · watch (financing)
The AI buildout is increasingly funded outside company books. On August 10 Nvidia and Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR announced platforms to mobilise more than $500 billion of third-party capital through vehicles that issue debt and lease compute, with Nvidia providing up to 25 percent financing support per deal; Nikkei had already counted about $1.65 trillion of special-purpose-vehicle debt tied to five US tech giants. Each layer of guarantees lets the next project borrow more.
What to watch: New special-purpose-vehicle debt, vendor financing and backstops from chipmakers, and any credit-rating action on AI-heavy issuers. Reports this week put Big Tech's second-quarter 'other income' past $160 billion on paper gains in private AI companies, more than double the roughly $69 billion of the quarter before, with Alphabet alone booking $97.9 billion; the concern is marks on OpenAI, Anthropic and SpaceX stakes flowing through as profit. SB Energy's IPO filing carries about $5.5 billion of warrants granted to OpenAI. Where it shows up: Oracle, Meta, data-center debt vehicles, and Nvidia.

A packaging rule with a levy of a few cents now costs a European maker about €1,150 a year in registrations.

Undercurrent

Alain Pannetrat of Lectronz, a marketplace for small electronics makers, wrote on August 24 that the EU's Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, which began to apply on August 12, obliges a one-person shop to register and appoint a paid representative in every member state it ships to: about €300 to €410 a year for France, €300 to €550 for Belgium, €200 for Germany, €350 for Austria, roughly €1,150 for four countries before a single parcel moves. 'For a French micro-entrepreneur, it makes more sense to ship products to the US than to ship to neighbouring Germany or Belgium.' The post reached 1,645 points on Hacker News, the week's second-highest. The same week the Italian Linux Society and FSFE launched refund4freedom.org, a guide to reclaiming the cost of an unwanted preinstalled Windows licence, with documented refunds of €40 to €129.

The payroll count loses another 178,000 private jobs on revision, the morning the Fed chair calls the labor market stable.stalling

Deep-dive of the week

The Bureau of Labor Statistics said on August 28 that its preliminary benchmark revision cuts total payrolls through March 2026 by 79,000, with private employment down 178,000 and government up 99,000; Goldman Sachs had forecast an upward revision of 50,000 to 450,000. At the same hour Kevin Warsh told Jackson Hole that 'labor markets are quite stable', that the 4.1 percent jobless rate 'has not changed much for a couple of years', and that he believed them 'consistent with full employment'. July payrolls had already fallen 23,000. Two federal actions in the same week narrowed legal labor supply: the State Department paused immigrant-visa appointments worldwide on August 26 while consular officers are retrained on the public-charge test, and DHS proposed a $103,265 fee on every cap-subject H-1B petition. Weekly jobless claims were 203,000. Pew reported that 54 percent of Americans in their forties have both a parent aged 65 or older and a child they are raising or supporting, and Statistics Korea logged a 24th straight month of rising births.

Geopolitics

US forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz on August 30, the first US attack on Iran in about a month, after Iranian forces were seen preparing to fire mines into the shipping lane; Iran answered with missiles at US sites in Jordan, eight of them intercepted, and on Monday claimed a drone attack on Al Minhad air base in Dubai that the UAE said it had shot down over its waters. Oil ended the week 5 percent lower anyway. In Nepal, a slab of ice fell off Langtang Lirung on the morning of August 26 and sent a surge down the valley that had killed 903 people and left 4,247 missing by August 31, among them 934 people from hydropower projects. Iceland voted 52.8 to 47.2 against reopening EU talks, Niger's junta says it put down a mutiny with help from Russia's Africa Corps, a Russian drone set off an ammunition depot beside a village west of Kyiv, and Washington announced a 100-year lease on 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan oil.

US strikes Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island; Iran sends drones at a base in Dubai (CNN)
The ceasefire broke on July 9 and the June memorandum lapsed on August 17; what changed on August 30 is that the two sides fired at each other again. Iran gave no confirmed casualty figure, though Tasnim cited unofficial reports of two killed, and its deputy foreign minister promised a 'decisive response'; on Monday the UAE confirmed downing a drone from Iran and denied Iranian claims that Al Minhad was hit. Nepal races to rescue hydropower workers as flood death toll reaches 903 (Al Jazeera)
The trigger was a glacier collapse, not a dam release or a cloudburst: a slab of ice fell 1.2 kilometres at 08:37 on August 26, dammed the river below, and the dam breached within minutes; seismometers recorded a magnitude 5.2 signal generated by the fall itself. As of August 31 Nepal counted 903 dead and 4,247 missing, Tibet 16 dead and 546 missing; 934 people working on about a dozen hydropower projects were listed missing, more than a hundred of them believed trapped in tunnels, and 431 megawatts is off the grid. China sent 2,100 rescuers and $33 million; the Gyirong border crossing is gone. The resource-scarcity chain this lane usually tracks ran in reverse here, with too much water arriving at once.

Iceland votes against restarting EU membership talks (BBC)
No took 52.84 percent to 47.16 on August 29, on turnout of 82.5 percent, in a referendum Prime Minister Kristrún Frostadóttir's coalition had promised after the 2024 election and pulled forward under the pressure of US tariffs and the Greenland crisis. Fisheries decided it: about 90 percent of fishing firms opposed, the three rural constituencies voted No by more than 60 percent, and the two Reykjavík seats voted Yes. The Yes campaign ran on the euro, with Iceland's policy rate at 8 percent against the ECB's 2.25. Accession talks, frozen since 2013, stay frozen; Iceland stays in the EEA and Schengen. The fragmentation regime's European edge held, in the one country that could have moved toward the bloc.

Niger's military government says it contained a mutiny; what next? (Al Jazeera)
Soldiers seized Air Base 101 and the airport in Niamey shortly after midnight on August 29, cut the state broadcaster and tried to reach the presidential palace, in a rising whose command structure is still unclear, involving soldiers from the Ouallam, Téra and Dosso fronts. The junta says it contained the rising, though General Tchiani's own whereabouts went unconfirmed during the fighting; Russia's ambassador confirmed Niger asked the Kremlin's Africa Corps for help putting it down, the Sahel alliance states and Algeria backed the junta, and Turkey called for restraint. Guinea-Bissau's junta held a referendum the next day on a constitution that would let the president dismiss governments and dissolve parliament; results are due September 1. In the Sahel the security guarantor is now Moscow, and it collected on the debt this week.

Resource control by lease is the energy regime's newest instrument, and the oil market read the announcement as supply.

A customs statute pulled a journalist's phone records without a judge, and California voted unanimously to keep open source out of age checks.

The wildcard · Privacy and digital rights

The Guardian reported on August 29 that after a judge twice refused search warrants for the YouTube accounts of Minneapolis journalist Georgia Fort and Don Lemon, DHS used a customs summons under 19 U.S.C. 1509 to take six months of Fort's T-Mobile records, more than 10,000 calls and texts, with no notice; the same tool reached the financial records of the Sunrise Movement, the SEIU and the Communications Workers of America, none of them charged. In Sacramento, AB 1856 passed the Senate 39-0 on August 26 and the Assembly 69-0 on August 27, taking GPL, MIT, BSD and Apache-licensed operating systems out of the Digital Age Assurance Act's duty to collect birth dates; it awaits Newsom's signature. In between, X Corp. sent cease-and-desist letters on August 24 that took nitter.net offline and reached xcancel.com, GrapheneOS said the Pixel 11 lacks the hardware memory tagging it depends on, and a Texas Tribune investigation found a $1 fee on car insurance has paid at least $30 million for about 3,200 Flock licence-plate cameras.

DHS is using an obscure customs law to obtain records on journalists, non-profits and unions (The Guardian, August 29)
Former DHS inspector general John Roth: 'This is not a customs case.' The Guardian says the frequency of 1509 summonses cannot be determined; the New York Times reported in February that DHS had served hundreds of administrative subpoenas on social media companies.

California AB 1856 passes, exempting open-source operating systems from the age-verification law (Phoronix)
The bill amends last year's Digital Age Assurance Act, which takes effect January 1, 2027; the EFF's read in May was 'one step forward, two steps back', since the same bill widens age-gating elsewhere.

X sends cease-and-desist to open-source project Nitter over alleged scraping (TechCrunch, August 25)
The letters, dated August 24, cite the Texas Harmful Access by Computer Act and the Lanham Act and demanded the repository and instances come down by 5pm ET the next day; nitter.net went offline, xcancel.com received a similar letter, and development stopped.

Lawmakers added $1 to car insurance policies. That money paid for Flock cameras (Texas Tribune, August 28)
The fee, passed unanimously in 2023, has raised about $81 million; grants ran from $7,000 for Bellmead to about $1.7 million for Dallas, and the governor paused state funding for local Flock grants this month.

MS Paint and Photos invisibly watermark even locally generated output with a GUID (xusheng.dev)
A server-issued 16-byte identifier is embedded in images generated on the local NPU, and the prompt is sent to a Microsoft moderation endpoint first; Paint refuses to save if the watermark fails. A Public First poll for the Center for Democracy and Technology, published August 30, found 89 percent of Britons say no one should read personal messages without a court order.

Tariff-dispersion shocks: the mean is the variance (NBER working paper, Schmitt-Grohé and Uribe)
At the six-digit tariff-line level the average US tariff and its dispersion move together on common shocks, which shift imports and the trade balance while barely moving output; temporary hikes cut imports and lift activity, while permanent ones cut imports and contract output before it expands.

Fear as a campaign tool: evidence from the 2014 US Ebola scare (NBER working paper, Campante, Depetris-Chauvin and Durante)
Republican candidates' Ebola messaging rose with negative sentiment and immigration framing regardless of proximity to actual cases, politicians' tweets predicted public attention rather than the reverse, and more fear messaging went with better results at the polls.
