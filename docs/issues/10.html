Signals to watch

Inference costs reverse toward custom silicon · active regime
After years of collapsing inference prices, the newest frontier models are raising them, pushing companies from general-purpose GPUs toward custom chips. AMD agreed on August 6 to buy Taalas, which burns a model's weights into the chip's own wiring: its HC1 encodes Llama 3.1 8B across 53 billion transistors and is quoted near 17,000 tokens per second per user at about 200 watts.
What to watch: Nvidia notified customers on August 22 of price rises above 15 percent on AI servers shipping early next year, blaming memory: server DRAM doubled in the first quarter and is now about a quarter of high-end rack cost, and Micron, SK Hynix and Samsung hold the pricing power. Watch whether the next price moves come from HBM contracts rather than GPU lists. Where it shows up: AMD, Marvell, Broadcom, and other custom-silicon makers.
Crypto leverage & dry powder · active (squeeze)
Leverage in crypto futures and stablecoin dry powder swing sharply, and extreme readings often precede reversals.
What to watch: Bitcoin ended the week up 24 percent at $78,000 after $1.44 billion of shorts liquidated on August 19, the kind of reversal this card exists to catch. Watch whether futures leverage and funding rates rebuild toward the extremes that preceded the spring drawdown, and whether the CLARITY Act contract, still at 16.5 cents, follows the price. Where it shows up: Bitcoin, Ether, and Coinbase.

Firefox is now the only major browser that will run uBlock Origin.

Undercurrent

Microsoft said on August 7 that Edge starts disabling Manifest V2 extensions for consumers this month and finishes by year-end, which leaves Firefox as the last major browser where uBlock Origin works; PCWorld's write-up was the week's top Hacker News story at 1,720 points, and Mozilla answered on Bluesky that its support 'isn't going anywhere'. The extension's filter maintainers said around August 10 they would stop chasing Facebook's ads, which the site now serves first-party with the word 'Sponsored' split character by character. Firefox for iOS shipped an experimental network-level ad blocker, off by default. And France's opt-in regime for telemarketing took effect August 11, with fines up to 375,000 euros per call for companies.

  • uBlock Origin is giving up the fight to keep ads off Facebook

Deep-dive of the week

Zambia voted on August 13, five years after Hakainde Hichilema took over a country in default. He restructured about $13 billion under the G20 Common Framework, finished a $1.7 billion IMF programme in January, and presides over Africa's best-performing currency and a record 890,346 tonnes of copper. With 135 of 226 constituencies counted he led Brian Mundubile by 1.54 million votes to 1.03 million. Turnout fell to 56 percent from about 70 in 2021, more than 70 percent of Zambians live on under $3 a day, and the count was suspended for six hours after a polling agent was beaten to death.

Geopolitics

The 60-day window in the June 17 Islamabad memorandum ran out on August 16 with no round of talks scheduled. Trump demanded compensation from Iran on August 10, a CENTCOM helicopter fired two Hellfire missiles into a Panama-flagged ship heading for an Iranian port on August 11, and Bessent promised on August 13 economic measures 'never seen'. Brent rose 6 percent on the week. In Gaza, the foreign ministers of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Egypt, Jordan, Turkey, Pakistan and Indonesia said on August 16 that Israel 'now bears responsibility for obstructing' peace, the same day Jared Kushner met Hamas's Khalil al-Hayya in Egypt. Colombia buried about 290 people from a magnitude 7.4 earthquake, and Washington moved its anti-cartel campaign onto land.

Araghchi said on August 15 that Tehran had 'not yet made a decision to restart negotiations' as the memorandum's 60 days expired; the same week the USS Abraham Lincoln passed 260 days at sea with one port stop, Navy Times reported sailors attempting to jump overboard, Trump said the deployment was 'not nearly long enough', and the USS George Washington was rerouted from the Pacific to relieve it.

Regional powers condemn Israel's rejection of Gaza plan (DW)
The eight-minister statement on August 16 said rejecting the roadmap 'directly threatens to derail' Trump's plan; Kushner and Board of Peace envoy Nickolay Mladenov were in Egypt meeting Egyptian, Qatari and Turkish mediators and Hamas, a week after Netanyahu said no withdrawal would happen until Hamas is 'genuinely' disarmed.

Colombia earthquake: search for survivors enters final phase (CBS News)
The magnitude 7.4 quake struck at 07:34 on August 10 near San José del Palmar in Chocó, on Abelardo de la Espriella's fourth day as president; by August 17 the toll was 289 dead and 4,187 injured across 450 municipalities, Cali counted 46 collapsed buildings and its mayor put reconstruction at about $3.2 billion, and an economic emergency was declared August 12. A magnitude 7.7 quake off Flores in Indonesia on August 15 killed at least 54.

Hegseth cautions that ICC may investigate US boat strikes in Latin America (Al Jazeera)

Parliament passes bill suspending sentences for PKK members (Bianet)
The 12-article law passed late on August 10 by 468 votes to 88 (Bianet counts 467); it suspends PKK-related prosecutions and sentences for five to ten years but takes effect only after the National Security Council certifies the group has disbanded and disarmed, and no weapons have been handed over yet. DEM's co-chair puts first-stage releases at 3,800 to 3,900.

Volatility measures how much the market is expected to move in the near term compared with the longer term, so a lower reading means less immediate stress. The yield curve is the gap between long-term and short-term government borrowing rates, and a steep curve usually points to expected growth rather than recession. When crypto is described as risk-off, investors are stepping back from the most speculative assets, which often serves as an early note of caution beneath a calm market.

The White House issues digital letters of marque against the scam economy.

The wildcard · The fraud and scam economy

A presidential memorandum on August 12, 'Expanding Capabilities to Combat Transnational Cyber-Enabled Crime', lets vetted private companies conduct surveillance and disruption operations against foreign criminal organisations, accessing systems 'without authorization from the owner or operator', under a DHS coordination centre with DOJ and DHS directors approving each operation, a bond of at least $1 million, and procedures due in 60 days. Nicholas Weaver called them 'digital letters of marque'. Two days earlier the FTC announced a court order freezing Credit Glory, 17 related companies and five principals alleged to have taken nearly $200 million since 2016 by buying Google search ads aimed at people looking up their debt collectors, some of them servicemembers who owed the Army and Air Force Exchange Service, then impersonating those collectors. On August 11, 404 Media found that Research Gold, which sold '100 percent human-written, never AI' systematic reviews from $1,900, listed eight AI-generated reviewers and answered its phones with AI.

Global monetary tightening on hold

The July inflation report did what the jobs report started. Headline CPI slowed to 3.5 percent from 3.7 as energy fell 1.5 percent on the month, core held at 2.8, and Polymarket's September hold went from 62.5 cents to 70.5 by Friday. Dedollarization advancing

The People's Bank of China appointed Deutsche Bank on August 10 as renminbi clearing bank for Europe, based in Frankfurt under a memorandum with the Bundesbank, the first non-Chinese bank in Europe to hold the designation; until now European clearing ran through units of Bank of China, ICBC and China Construction Bank. The renminbi's share of SWIFT payments in June was 3.10 percent, fifth place, against 50.10 for the dollar, and the PBoC bought gold for a 21st straight month in July, adding 640,000 ounces to 76.08 million, its largest monthly purchase since October 2023.
