Undercurrent

Three sites built 215,128 'best software' pages for machines to read, and Pew finds one web page in ten now carries the marks of AI.

A September 2 report from Trellner traced 215,128 'best software' pages across wifitalents.com, worldmetrics.org and gitnux.org, registered between December 2023 and May 2024 on shared infrastructure and templates, two of which title their pages 'Facts & Grounding Page'; of 7,534 URLs Perplexity cited across 380 software-category queries, 59.8 percent ranked outside the top 100,000 sites. 'There are not 215,128 software categories.' Pew's August 20 study of about 490,000 Common Crawl pages found that 10 percent 'show significant signs of AI authorship', and more than a third of those published since ChatGPT. Bryan Cantrill's September 5 essay put the reader's side: to use a model to write 'is to void the social contract between writer and reader', and readers 'can absolutely tell'. Debian's members chose the counterpoint on August 29, a 'responsible use' policy under which 'the use of a generative AI tool does not diminish the contributor's responsibility for the work they submit.'

The wider world

Deep-dive of the weeksteady

Google keeps its ad exchange. The second monopoly ruling against it in a year ends with conduct rules and a sealed opinion.

On September 2 Judge Leonie Brinkema of the Eastern District of Virginia rejected all three structural remedies the Justice Department had sought in the ad-tech case, including the sale of Google's AdX exchange, and accepted most of the proposed behavioural remedies 'as modified by this Court'. Her opinion is under seal; the parties have until September 16 to propose redactions and until October 2 to file a proposed final judgment, so nobody outside the case yet knows what Google must do. It is the same shape as Judge Mehta's search remedy of September 2025, which refused to order Chrome sold. The search case is on cross-appeal at the D.C. Circuit; Google has said it will appeal the ad-tech liability ruling, which would go to the Fourth Circuit, once final judgment is entered. The same week the Supreme Court's interim docket took four election disputes in five filings, a district judge blocked the second birthright-citizenship order, and the Pentagon said Anthropic remains a supply-chain risk despite an August 27 judgment saying the designation was unlawful.

Key factBoth of the Justice Department's monopolisation wins against Google, Judge Mehta's in September 2025 and Judge Brinkema's on September 2, ended with the judge refusing divestiture; the ad-tech order sets no implementation date, names no trustee and fixes no duration until the parties file a proposed final judgment by October 2.

What the readings mean

Volatility measures how much the market is expected to move in the near term compared with the longer term, so a lower reading means less immediate stress. The yield curve is the gap between long-term and short-term government borrowing rates, and a steep curve usually points to expected growth rather than recession. When crypto is described as risk-off, investors are stepping back from the most speculative assets, which often serves as an early note of caution beneath a calm market.

The wildcard · Education

China's largest graduating class meets its highest July youth jobless rate in three years, and a global bank makes AI use a condition of a first job.

The New York Times reported on September 6 on the 12.7 million graduates entering China's workforce this year, a cohort the education ministry had announced last November as about 4 percent larger than 2025's 12.22 million, in a labour market where the July unemployment rate for 16-to-24-year-olds not in school was 17.9 percent, up from 14.9 in June and the highest July reading in three years (Caixin, August 20). The same day the Financial Times reported that UBS will require its 2027 junior banking hires to show they can use AI, among the first big banks to make that explicit. In the United States, NAFSA's August outlook projects up to 111,000 fewer international students this autumn and up to $3.4 billion in lost spending, driven by graduate enrolment; Education Week counts at least 38 states plus the District of Columbia requiring districts to restrict phones as school returns; and an NBER paper puts a year of ordinary air-pollution exposure at 14 to 19 percent of a year's typical test-score growth.

A record 12.7 million graduates enter China's workforce in 2026 as AI adds uncertainty to an oversaturated job market (New York Times, September 6, as summarised by Techmeme)
The figure itself dates from the education ministry's November 2025 announcement; what is new is the jobless rate it meets, 17.9 percent for 16-to-24-year-olds in July, against a 5.2 percent urban rate overall. The August figure is due in mid-September.

UBS demands new junior bankers show proficiency in AI (Financial Times, September 6)
The FT report, as summarised by Techmeme and The Next Web, says graduates and interns joining the global banking and markets business in 2027 must show they can use AI to improve outcomes and efficiency.

Fall 2026 international student enrollment outlook (NAFSA, August 11)
A mid-projection of 1,104,912 international students from 1,168,602 in 2025, 'up to 111,000 fewer', 'up to $3.4 billion' in lost spending and nearly 40,000 fewer jobs; 'Graduate-level enrollment declines are the primary driver of economic losses.'

School cellphone bans expand as students return to class (Patch, July 28)
'At least 38 states and the District of Columbia now require local school districts to ban or restrict student cellphone use in schools,' by Education Week's updated tracker; New York's bell-to-bell rule is the largest state's version.

Air pollution and learning (NBER working paper 35670, Behrer, Goodman and co-authors)
From the abstract: 'A year of observed pollution exposure reduces learning by 0.04-0.06 standard deviations, or 14-19% of typical annual score growth.'
